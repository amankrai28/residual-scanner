How it works — and what it honestly is

1 · Fair value. A gradient-boosted hedonic model (scikit-learn) is trained on Cambridge arm's-length sales since 2018 — living area, beds, baths, land, year built, condition, grade, style, zoning, assessor map sheet and more — with sale timing as a feature. On held-out sales it lands within — of the actual price at the median, and within 15% on — of homes. Every residential parcel then gets a model "fair value" as of 2026.

2 · Mispricing. For every 2024–2026 sale, the scanner computes the residual: (fair value − sale price) / fair value. A big positive residual means the home traded below what its physical fundamentals predict — the signal a manual browse can't see.

3 · Momentum. Renovation (addition/alteration) permits are aggregated per street: trailing 24 months vs. the prior 24. Rising permit velocity is a leading indicator of neighborhood reinvestment. The composite score blends discount (65%) and street momentum (35%).

Honesty notes. Discounts beyond ~40% are flagged, not celebrated — in Cambridge they are almost always deed-restricted affordable units or non-arm's-length transfers, and treating them as alpha would be naive. This edition scores closed sales (what sold cheap vs. fundamentals) and an under-assessed watchlist (long-held homes whose model value runs well ahead of assessment, on heating streets — the knock-on-the-door list). A live-listings feed plugs into the same model unchanged. A model residual is a screen, not an appraisal: it can't see interior condition on sale day, deal terms, or deed restrictions.
